Phase 1 · final version

The amx cockpit

Nineteen renderings on a strict character grid, covering both axes, both modes, both themes, and a phone at 46 columns. Every dimension is an integer number of cells; nothing uses a radius, a shadow, a gradient, a second type size or a hover state. The two axes share one row template and differ by exactly one 10-cell column, which is the column the state word lives in when the group heading is no longer saying it.

What carries the hierarchy
Case and weight. What carries the hierarchy
Position, then colour, in that order. Needs input is always the first group and always the top of the screen, so at forty agents the answer to the one question is in the same place it is at three. Its four rows are the only bold names on the wall and the badge above restates the number in a reversed run. Thirty-six other rows wear no colour beyond their state glyph.
03

Dir axis · the same fleet under paths · 100 columns · dark

{{ sp.t }}
What carries the hierarchy
One inserted column, and dimming inside the heading. The path heading keeps the rule and the right-aligned count of the state heading, so the two axes are visibly the same document; what changes is that the parent directories are dim and only the last segment is bold, which gives a left-heavy string of varying length a consistent bright end. A long path elides in the middle, never at the end, because the end is the part that identifies it. Every row then grows a dim 8-cell state word in the gap the heading used to fill — the same information, moved down a level.

What carries the hierarchy
The same structure, with the summary column absorbing every cut. Below 100 columns the name column drops from 22 cells to 16, the dial labels drop to their values, the hint row loses its two least-used pairs, and paths elide harder. The state word column does not shrink: eight cells is what the longest state word needs, and a truncated state word would be a lie. Nothing wraps and no band changes its line count, so the screen you learn wide is the screen you get narrow.
05

The card, open on a waiting agent · dark

{{ sp.t }}
What carries the hierarchy
Indentation. The card is not a box; it is a spine hanging off the row's own state glyph in column 2, closed with ╰, its text starting at the name column. It cannot be mistaken for belonging to another row, it needs no border cells, and it pushes the list down rather than covering it — closing it is a change of line count, not a dissolve. It does not repeat the question the row above already carries in full; it adds the command being asked about, in bold, and where it is running, in dim.
06

Input mode · the wall live behind it · dark

{{ sp.t }}
What carries the hierarchy
A labelled edge, and two lines that look nothing like each other. A full-width rule breaks the composer away from the wall and carries the word TASK and the sentence that matters — letters are text until esc — at its left end. The promoted permission dial sits at the other end of that same rule, reversed in the accent colour and unlabelled — the one setting that changes what the agent may do without asking, on the edge you cannot miss, costing no line of its own. Below the rule, two lines and nothing else: the typed text, bold, with a block cursor, the only bold text left while the mode is on; then the hint row's ordinary grammar, bold key and dim gloss, with shift+tab among them because it is what cycles the dial above. The entire wall — rows, headings, counts, the header dials — goes dim at the same instant, so the mode change is visible in peripheral vision without reading a word.

What carries the hierarchy
Two ruled columns and one indent step. Family names reuse the wall's heading treatment exactly — uppercase, bold, dim rule, right-aligned count — so the overlay is legibly the same document as the thing behind it. Keys are bold in a 12-cell column, glosses are dim in 44, which is wide enough that not one entry elides. The columns are balanced by entry count, 15 against 14, rather than by family. No colour at all, because nothing here is waiting on anyone.

What carries the hierarchy
Weight takes over from dim. Nothing in the design asks a colour to be a particular hue — only to be the theme's waiting, done, failed, stopped or accent. Dim is a weaker signal on paper than on ink, so on light the bold of the headings and the waiting names does proportionally more of the work, and the two reversed runs in the design — the waiting badge, and the permission dial while you are typing — invert to paper-on-ink and stay the loudest things on the screen.
10

Phone · 46 columns · outside the stated envelope

The brief sets 80 columns as the floor. A terminal app on a phone in portrait is about 46 columns at a readable size, so this is below it and I am flagging it rather than smuggling it in. What it buys: the cockpit is the screen you check when you are not at your desk, and checking is exactly the cheap half of the job — is anything waiting on me needs the badge and the first group, not the summaries. Everything here is the same grammar with one field per row instead of three.

What carries the hierarchy
Subtraction, and one inversion. Each row keeps its one-line rule but shows a single text field: the question on a waiting row, the name on every other, because a name you cannot act on is worth less than a question you can. The summary is not truncated to nothing, it is moved to the card, which is the thing a thumb opens anyway. Band 1 becomes two lines — identity and badge, then the counts — since 46 cells cannot hold both, and the dials keep their values and lose their labels. Input mode drops its key line entirely: a phone keyboard already shows return, and two rows of bindings on a 46-cell screen is more instruction than the mode needs. Headings, rules, counts, spine and reversed badge are unchanged, so this is recognisably the same screen at half the width.

Rationale, with cell budgets

Band 1 against band 2
Facts and dials are now different kinds of line rather than two lines of the same weight. Band 1 is the only line carrying bold text and the only line carrying a reversed run. Band 2 hangs off it on a └ in column 0 — one cell — with dim labels and accent values. A branch glyph reads as subordinate without a word of explanation.
The waiting badge
The count of waiting agents is set in reverse video at the right end of band 1: ␣N␣WAITING␣, 11 cells at one digit. At zero it is replaced by the dim words nothing waiting in the same position. This is the element the one-second test rests on; everything else is secondary.
Row geometry, state axis
Prefix 4 cells, unchanged from today: unread or pin mark (1), space (1), state glyph (1), space (1). Then name 22, two spaces, summary fills, two spaces, age right-aligned in 4. The name column is 22 rather than 26 so that both axes can share it.
Row geometry, dir axis
Identical, with a 10-cell block inserted between name and summary: state word 8, two spaces. Eight cells is the longest state word (starting). The summary column absorbs the whole cost, so the name, the age and the group count stay in the columns they occupy on the other axis — switching axis moves one boundary, not the table.
Path headings
Parents dim, last segment bold, then the same dim rule and right-aligned count as a state heading. Over-long paths elide in the middle — first segment, …, last two segments — because the tail is what identifies a worktree. Paths are not uppercased: a path is a name, not a word, and uppercasing it would make it less readable, not more heading-like. The rule and the count do the heading work instead.
Which axis you are on
Said once, in the hint row, as the gloss on its own binding: ctrl+s by state or ctrl+s by dir. Zero extra lines, and it sits on the key that changes it.
Group headings
Uppercase, bold, one space, a dim rule out to two cells short of the age column, then the group's count right-aligned in that column. Case plus rule makes a heading without a size change; the count in the age column means the right margin is one alignment rather than two.
Vertical rhythm
One blank line before every group heading, none inside a group, one after band 2, and the hint row pinned to the last line. Slack collects above the pinned hint row, so the screen has the same shape at 3 agents and at 40.
The card
│ in column 2, directly under the row's state glyph, closed with ╰, text from column 4 — the name column. 3 columns of indent, no border cells, 9 lines.
Input mode
A labelled rule carrying the mode word at one end and the promoted permission dial, reversed, at the other; then two indented lines — bold typed text, then bold key and dim gloss. Plus one global change: every row, every heading, every count and both dial lines drop to dim for the duration. Cost: 4 lines at the bottom replacing the 1-line hint row, no extra columns, and 16 cells of reverse video for the dial.
Help overlay
Two columns of 58 cells at 120 wide: 2 indent, 12 key, 44 gloss. Every gloss fits; nothing elides. Below 100 columns it becomes a single column of the same rows and pages with pgdn — the two-column layout is not squeezed, it is abandoned, because a 34-cell gloss column would put the truncation back.
Phone, at 46 columns
Below the brief's 80-column floor, so it is a proposal rather than a deliverable. Row prefix and age column are unchanged; the name and summary columns collapse into one 36-cell field holding whichever of the two the row's state makes worth reading. Band 1 takes a second line for the counts. No new glyphs, no new colours, no new devices.
Two deliberate deviations
The unread mark is painted in the waiting colour only on waiting rows and dim elsewhere: at forty rows, amber dots on finished work would compete with the thing that colour is for. And ⏹ is drawn here as ■, because the specified glyph is double-width in several common terminal fonts and would break the grid by one cell. Both are yours to overrule.
